Somatic Symptom Disorders

Primary Gain, Secondary Gain & La Belle Indifference

Primary Gain: Internal

  • A benefit that protects from internal anxiety, emotional symptoms or conflicts.
  • Produces internal motivators.

Secondary Gain: External

  • Relieves the individual from external expected responsibilities.
  • Produces external motivators.
  • Examples: more attention and sympathy · missing work · admission to a hospital or psychiatric facility · financial assistance · psychiatric or other disability compensation · prescription medications · avoiding a jail sentence.

La Belle Indifference

A lack of concern or emotional response despite having a serious medical illness or physical symptoms related to a health condition. Most often associated with conversion disorder.

Clinical Manifestations

DisorderClinical Manifestations
Somatic symptom disorder (SSD)
  • Actual, persistent and distressing physical symptoms (pain, fatigue, digestive issues) that cause significant anxiety.
  • Often without a clear medical cause.
  • Preoccupation with health, with excessive time and energy devoted to symptoms.
Illness anxiety disorder (IAD)
  • Intense fear or worry about having or acquiring a serious illness, despite few or no actual physical symptoms.
  • Constantly checks the body for signs of illness and seeks reassurance from doctors despite normal results, or avoids medical situations.
  • Hyperfocus on health-related information, such as medical websites.
Conversion disorder (functional neurological symptom disorder)
  • Neurological symptoms (paralysis, tremors, non-epileptic seizures, vision problems) that are inconsistent with or cannot be explained by medical tests.
  • Often triggered by stress or trauma.
Psychological factors affecting medical conditionPsychological factors, such as stress, anxiety or depression, that worsen a physical medical condition such as chronic pain, asthma or heart disease.
Factitious disorder (imposed on self or others)
  • Intentionally pretending, lying about, faking or producing physical or psychological symptoms to assume the sick role and gain things such as attention or sympathy.
  • May involve falsifying medical histories, manipulating test results or intentionally harming oneself.
MalingeringIntentionally exaggerating or fabricating physical or psychological symptoms for external gain: avoiding work, admission to a hospital or psychiatric facility, financial compensation, or prescription medications.

Telling Similar Disorders Apart

SSD Versus Illness Anxiety Disorder

Somatic Symptom Disorder

The focus is the distress caused by actual physical symptoms.

Illness Anxiety Disorder

The focus is the fear, worry and anxiety about the possibility of being seriously ill, despite minimal or no symptoms.

Factitious Disorder Versus Malingering

Both involve intentional exaggeration or fabrication. The difference is the motivation behind it.

Factitious Disorder

Motivated by internal incentives (primary gain), such as seeking nurturance and sympathy for being ill. Also called the need to assume the sick role.

Malingering

Motivated by external incentives (secondary gain).

Impact on the Individual

DisorderImpact on the Individual
Somatic symptom disorder
  • Chronic anxiety and worry about health, interfering with daily life.
  • Frequent doctor visits and unnecessary medical tests, with a high emotional and financial burden.
  • Impaired social and occupational functioning.
Illness anxiety disorder
  • Persistent fear or worry causing significant distress and disruption of daily activities.
  • Avoids certain activities or places for fear of illness.
  • Can lead to doctor shopping and high health-related costs, or to avoiding healthcare altogether.
Conversion disorder
  • Sudden disability, with impaired mobility and independence.
  • Emotional distress from the sudden onset and the lack of a clear medical explanation.
  • Social and occupational disruption.
Psychological factors affecting medical condition
  • Poor health outcomes from the interaction between psychological and physical symptoms.
  • Difficulty managing multiple health concerns at once.
  • Impaired quality of life.
Factitious disorder
  • Emotional distress stemming from the need for attention or care.
  • Trust issues in relationships and among healthcare providers because of the deception.
  • Risk of unnecessary medical treatments and complications.
Malingering
  • Distrust and breakdown in relationships, especially with healthcare providers.
  • Legal consequences if fraudulent behavior is detected.
  • Potential for unnecessary treatments or accommodations based on false information.

Treatment

Across the Somatic Symptom Disorders

  • Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT): the primary and most effective treatment. Addresses the relationship between irrational thinking and emotional and behavioral distress, and helps the individual reframe thoughts about their health and reduce anxiety.
  • Mindfulness-based therapy and stress reduction techniques.
  • Pharmacotherapy, typically antidepressants and anxiolytics: not the primary intervention. Relieves specific symptoms, and is used more when anxiety or depression co-occurs.
  • Psychoeducation is important in all of these disorders. It helps individuals understand the connection between their physical symptoms and psychological states.
  • Interprofessional collaborative care is often needed, especially for conversion disorder and psychological factors affecting medical condition.
DisorderTreatments
Somatic symptom disorderCBT for health anxiety and dysfunctional thoughts · mindfulness and relaxation techniques to reduce stress · antidepressants, such as SSRIs, for underlying anxiety or depression.
Illness anxiety disorderCBT to reduce health anxiety and correct misinterpretations of bodily sensations · psychoeducation about health anxiety · antidepressants or anxiolytics to manage anxiety.
Conversion disorderPhysical therapy and rehabilitation to help restore function · CBT to address stress or trauma · psychodynamic therapy to explore unresolved psychological conflict · stress management techniques to prevent symptom exacerbation.
Psychological factors affecting medical conditionIntegrated care approach combining medical and psychological treatment · CBT for stress and coping strategies · relaxation techniques and mindfulness to reduce the impact of psychological stress on physical health.
Factitious disorderPsychotherapy, particularly CBT or psychodynamic therapy, to uncover underlying emotional distress · long-term therapy for the psychological need to gain attention through illness · family therapy if symptoms are imposed on others.
MalingeringPsychiatric evaluation to confirm the diagnosis and underlying motivations · legal or social intervention if external gains are discovered · in some cases, behavioral therapy for the underlying motives.

Nursing Interventions

For Patients with Somatic Symptom Disorders

  • Establish a therapeutic relationship: build trust and empathize with the individual.
  • Reduce health dependency: collaborate with the healthcare team and coordinate appointments. Help the individual develop a structured plan for follow-up appointments, and encourage involvement in both medical and psychiatric care so physiological and psychological needs are both addressed.
  • Provide education on the disorder.
  • Encourage realistic expectations and set reasonable therapeutic goals.
  • Teach coping strategies and encourage their use: relaxation, meditation, biofeedback.
  • Assist with medication management.
